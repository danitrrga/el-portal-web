<objective>
Translate the first three sections of the home page: `Hero`, `VCDSection` and
`McpIntegrationSection`.

Purpose: the home page is the surface a negotiated Spanish visitor lands on
first, and the hero H1 is the single most typographically constrained string on
the site. Phase 5 tuned its clamp to measured values
(`clamp(1.625rem,3.571vw+0.911rem,2.625rem) md:clamp(42px,4.2vw,58px)`) and that
value is frozen in both locales — Spanish must fit it by being written to fit,
not by the type scale bending.

`VCDSection` is where the derived glossary earns its place: Version, Cycle and
Day are the product's core primitives and the app already ships
`Versiones` / `Ciclo` / `Dia` for them. Inventing alternatives here would be
visible to any reader who then opens the app.

Output: a `home` namespace in both locales, with English rendering unchanged.
</objective>

containment sweep for `/es` at 320, 360, 390 and 430 and confirm no text is wider
than its own box.

The text-expansion contract governs what counts as a problem. A Spanish heading
wrapping to MORE lines than English is acceptable and expected — `text-balance`
and `max-w-*` exist to reflow. Text wider than its own box is not acceptable.
When a Spanish string does not fit, shorten the copy; the register's infinitive
constructions are naturally compact. Shrinking a clamp to make a long string fit
on one line is explicitly not an acceptable fix, and neither is a locale-specific
type scale — two rhythm systems for one layout is itself a design regression.

Both hero CTA labels sit inside `text-nowrap` spans, so they grow rather than
wrap. `Abrir El Portal` and `Leer la metodologia` are comparable length to their
English originals, but confirm the button row at 320px anyway; below `md:` the
buttons stack `flex-col`, so the pressure is a desktop-width concern.
